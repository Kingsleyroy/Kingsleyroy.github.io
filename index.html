How I work

The same six steps whether it's a council repair process, a studio's sales pipeline or a live event.

  1. Sit with the people doing the job

    Requirements come from the person filling in the form at 8am, not from a diagram. I record what they actually do, including the workarounds.

    Interviews, shadowing, requirements notes

  2. Map the as-is process

    Every step, hand-off, approval and waiting point written down, so it's clear where work stalls and who owns each stage.

    Process mapping

  3. Measure it before changing it

    Volumes, cycle times, rework and the hours spent on manual updates. Without a baseline there's nothing to prove afterwards.

    Excel, SQL, Power BI

  4. Design the to-be process

    One source of truth, a status that drives the workflow, automatic notifications, and a rule for every exception the team raised.

    Solution design, data model

  5. Build it in low-code tools

    Microsoft Forms, SharePoint and Power Automate, or Zoho Creator, CRM, Sign and Books, built on licences the organisation already holds.

    Power Automate, SharePoint, Zoho

  6. Test, hand over, then check the numbers

    Run it alongside the old way, fix what breaks, write the short guide, train the team, then compare against the baseline.

    UAT, documentation, reporting

Case study

Kent County Council, Family Hub Programme

Automated facilities management

A no-code Microsoft 365 system that takes an office repair from the first report to the contractor's closure email, with no manual updates in between.

My role
Designed, built and rolled out the whole system
Tools
Microsoft Forms, SharePoint, Power Automate, Outlook shared mailbox
Users
Hub staff, business support, managers and the facilities contractor

The problem

Repairs were reported by email and tracked in a shared spreadsheet. Business support had to forward every request to the facilities contractor, wait for a reply, copy the job reference into the sheet and chase for updates.

  • Manual entries were often missed, mistyped or out of date.
  • Out-of-date records caused duplicate call-outs, so engineers sometimes visited twice for the same job.
  • Nobody could see the live status of a job without asking business support.

How the system fits together

Microsoft Forms is the front door, a SharePoint list is the single source of truth, and three Power Automate flows move each job forward by reading and sending email from one central shared mailbox.

Microsoft FormsStaff report a repair
SharePoint listOffice Repair Requests
Flow 1Emails the contractor
Flow 2Captures the call reference
Flow 3Closes the job and tells staff
Manager viewLive status by job

How I built it

  1. Designed the SharePoint list

    Created an "Office Repair Requests" list with columns for title, repair type, location, description, urgency, requested by, status and call reference. Choice columns keep repair types and urgency consistent, and Status drives the whole workflow.

    SharePoint

  2. Built a simple form for staff

    Staff report issues through a Microsoft Form called "Skanska Reporting", so they never touch the list directly. A Power Automate flow triggers on each new response, reads the answers and creates a matching list item.

    Microsoft Forms, Power Automate

  3. Flow 1: send the request to the contractor

    Triggered when a new item is created. It sends a formatted email to the contractor's helpdesk from the team's central shared mailbox, so replies come back to one place, then sets Status to "Email sent".

    Multi-select choice fields came through as raw text like ["Plumbing"], so I cleaned them with an expression before adding them to the email:

    replace(replace(string(triggerBody()?['RepairType/Value']),'["',''),'"]','')

    Power Automate, Outlook shared mailbox

  4. Flow 2: capture the job reference

    Watches the shared mailbox for new emails from the contractor. A condition checks whether the body contains "Call reference". If it does, the flow extracts the reference number into a variable, writes it to the matching list item and changes Status to "Job in progress".

    Variables are initialised at the top of the flow and set inside the condition's "Yes" branch, because Power Automate doesn't allow initialising a variable inside a condition.

    Power Automate, expressions, variables

  5. Flow 3: close the job

    Looks for the contractor's closure emails, which follow the pattern "Call reference: 000000 - CLOSED". It finds the list item with that reference, sets Status to "Completed" and emails the person who reported the repair.

    Power Automate, SharePoint

  6. Gave managers a live view

    Added a Manager Dashboard view on the list, grouped by Status, so anyone can see what's been sent, what's in progress and what's done without asking.

    SharePoint views

Results

Noneof the manual spreadsheet updates that caused errors
Nomore duplicate engineer visits from stale records
Livejob status for staff and managers
Lesschasing and admin for business support

Case study

Studio 31, Chennai

Wedding studio, fully digital

An end-to-end Zoho system that runs a wedding and events studio from the first website enquiry to the final download, replacing spreadsheets, manual follow-ups and scattered cloud folders.

My role
Started in sales, became production manager and led the move to digital
Tools
Zoho Creator, Zoho CRM, Zoho Sign, Zoho Meeting, Zoho Books, Deluge
Teams connected
Sales, production, crew, finance, post-production and clients

The problem

Every stage of the business ran on Excel, phone calls and reminders people had to remember. Enquiries were logged by hand, follow-ups slipped, contracts went back and forth over email, and footage was shared through several cloud apps.

  • Sales had no reliable way to see which leads were owned or due a follow-up.
  • Production often heard about a booking late, with incomplete client details.
  • Finance and post-production had no clear signal of when an event was finished.
  • Clients had to ask for updates and chase for their final files.

How the system fits together

Zoho Creator became the operations hub, with a dashboard for each role. Each hand-off between teams is triggered by a record changing stage, using Creator workflows and Deluge scripts, with other Zoho apps plugged in for contracts, meetings, payments and post-production.

Website formClient enquiry
Zoho CreatorLeads, orders, crew, dashboards
Zoho SignClient agreement
Zoho MeetingPlanning call and minutes
Zoho BooksInvoice and payment
Zoho CRMPost-production tracking
Client dashboardLive status and downloads

The process, step by step

  1. Enquiry capture

    The enquiry form on the studio website is connected to Zoho Creator. Each submission creates a lead record with the client's details, event type, date and venue, and lands in an unassigned leads queue.

    Website form, Zoho Creator

  2. Lead self-assignment

    Salespeople pick up leads from the queue. Claiming a lead sets them as the owner and moves the record onto their personal sales dashboard, so every lead has one clear owner.

    Zoho Creator dashboards, Deluge

  3. Automated nurturing

    Based on the lead's stage, scheduled workflows create email follow-up reminders and call reminders for the owner, so no enquiry is left waiting and client questions get a prompt answer.

    Zoho Creator schedules, email notifications

  4. Order form on closing the deal

    Once a client confirms, the salesperson submits an order form with the package, event dates, venue and agreed value. Submitting it converts the lead into an order and routes it to the production manager's dashboard.

    Zoho Creator forms and workflows

  5. Digital agreement

    At the same time, the client is emailed the service agreement through Zoho Sign, pre-filled from the order. The signed status is written back to the order, and the project officially starts once it's signed.

    Zoho Sign

  6. Planning meeting and minutes

    The production manager gets a meeting reminder and runs the client planning call on Zoho Meeting. The call is recorded, and the minutes of the meeting are emailed to the client straight afterwards.

    Zoho Meeting

  7. Crew assignment and briefing

    From the dashboard, the production manager uses an assignment link to pick the crew for each event. The assigned crew receive the minutes and the client brief, so everyone arrives with the same details.

    Zoho Creator

  8. Event completion

    After the event, the crew mark it as completed on the order. That single update notifies finance and moves the order to billing.

    Zoho Creator

  9. Invoicing and payment

    Finance raises the invoice and sends the client a payment request from Zoho Books. When the payment is received it's recorded in Zoho Books, and the paid status syncs back to the order.

    Zoho Books

  10. Post-production

    Confirmed payment hands the project to post-production, tracked in Zoho CRM. The team sets a delivery deadline and allocates the footage to editors, with progress visible to everyone.

    Zoho CRM

  11. Delivery to the client

    When the edits are ready, they're published to the client's dashboard, where the client can follow progress and download the final deliverables.

    Client dashboard in Zoho Creator

Results

18%fewer labour hours across the business
20%rise in overall productivity
1 yearto recover the cost of the transition through automation
Liveupdates for clients through their own dashboard

Sales, production and post-production now work from the same records, so hand-offs are visible, follow-ups happen on time and client issues are picked up quickly.

Case study

Invictas Cricket Association, Canterbury

Invictas club website

A landing page that brings fixtures, live scores, club news and player feedback together in one place for a new T20 club.

My role
We founded the club and I am chairman. I planned and built the site as a volunteer project
Built with
Single-page website, hosted on Vercel
Audience
Players, committee, sponsors and new members

The problem

The club's only online presence was a basic Play-Cricket page. Scores, news and updates were spread across different places, and players had no simple way to share ideas or concerns with the committee.

How I built it

  1. Planned what the club needed

    Listed what players, the committee and sponsors look for most: upcoming fixtures, live scores, club news and a way to get in touch.

  2. Designed the club's look

    Built the page around the Invictas identity so it feels like an established club, not a default template.

  3. Built the key sections

    Fixtures, live scores, club activities and news all sit on one page, with a feedback and suggestion box so players can reach the committee directly.

  4. Published and shared it

    Deployed the site on Vercel and shared the link with players and supporters as the club's main home online.

    Vercel

Results

  • Raised the club's standard and how it presents itself to players and sponsors.
  • Live scores and club activities now sit under one roof.
  • The feedback box has improved communication between the committee and players.
